# Scales narrow the pool; the walk orders it

Scales enter practice as a note filter on the pool — Chromatic, or Major or Minor in one key — beside the session scope, not as a game of their own. A key therefore composes with every item source: the scheduler draws due items of F major, "First N" takes the learning order's first N inside the key, and the walk lists every item of the key in pitch order. The walk is the third item source ("Up and down"): every scoped item, up and back down, one layout at a time. With no key it is the chromatic walk the chromatic-walk spec sketched, which closes that spec's open questions: the walk lives on the practice setup, records through the seam (ADR 0004), covers the layout's whole range, runs both ways, skips a layout's missing semitones silently (it lists the buttons that exist), and treats a twin pitch as one stop with the engine's follow-up asking for the other button. The key fixes the spelling — F major names B♭ — so the Accidentals choice steps aside under a key. A spelling is a table of twelve names, not a ♯/♭ switch, so a key names its naturals too: F♯ major's seventh degree is E♯, never F. The six-sharp minor goes by D♯ minor, not E♭ minor: E♭ minor needs C♭, and C♭4 sounds B3, so the written octave would disagree with the sounding one the note game's octave buttons name; E♯4 sounds F4 and keeps its number. Rejected: a separate "Scale" item source with its own order switch (a third source of items that could not be scheduled); up-only and down-only walks (narrow the scope instead); several keys in one run (the union of keys is the chromatic scale, and walking twelve keys is far too long).
